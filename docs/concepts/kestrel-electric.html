How a job runs

Five steps, and you know which one you are on.

Electrical work is invisible once the covers go back on, which is exactly why the process has to be visible while it happens.

  1. You describe it, we ask three questions

    What is happening, how old the house is, and a photo of the panel with the door open. Half of quotes are given from that.

  2. The quote, in writing

    Labour, materials, and the permit fee as separate lines, so you can see what you are paying for and what you could leave out.

  3. A window you can plan around

    Two hours, morning or afternoon, and a text when the van is twenty minutes away. If we are running late you hear before the window closes, not after.

  4. The work, and the walk through

    Covers off, covers on, and a five minute tour of what changed and where the new breakers are labelled.

  5. Inspection, then the invoice

    On permitted work the county inspects, then you pay. The invoice matches the quote unless you agreed to a change in writing.

If something changes. Old wiring hides surprises. When one turns up, work stops, you get a photo and a revised number, and nothing continues until you say so.

Before you call

The questions people ring with.

Do I need a permit for this?

For a new circuit, a panel, a charger or a generator connection, yes, and we pull it. For swapping a like for like outlet or fixture, usually no. If you are told a panel can be done without one, that is a reason to call someone else.

Can you quote from a photo?

Often. A clear photo of the panel with the door open, the label inside it, and the spot where the work goes tells us most of what we need. If it does not, the visit is free when you go ahead with the job.

My breaker trips and the last electrician just replaced it. Why is it still tripping?

Because the breaker was doing its job. A breaker that trips is protecting the wire behind it from something further along, and swapping the breaker treats the alarm instead of the fire. The diagnostic visit finds the something.
