The shop

One chair, so one customer at a time.

There is no waiting area because nobody waits. The chair is yours for the time booked, and the next person arrives when you are getting up.

  1. Walk in until two. After two the chair is by appointment, and a walk in gets the next gap if there is one.
  2. Say it in plain words. A bit shorter than last time is a perfectly good plan. A photo is better. A number on the guard is best.
  3. Phones on silent in the chair. Yours and the barber's. The playlist is the shop's and it is quiet.
  4. Kids on Saturday mornings. Before eleven, when nobody is waiting and a first haircut can be stopped and started.
  5. Running late is fine, say so. Ten minutes late still gets the cut. Twenty gets whatever fits before the next person.

The work

Three things the barber would say if you asked.

Nothing here is a philosophy. It is the answer to the questions that come up in the chair most weeks.

A good fade is not the shortest one

It is the one where you cannot find the line. That takes more passes with more guards than most shops allow time for, which is why the fade here is forty minutes and priced like it.

The first two minutes are the haircut

What you say before the cape goes on decides everything after. If you are not sure, the barber will ask about the last cut you liked, and the one you did not.

Product is optional and not for sale here

You will be told what was used and where to buy it if you ask. Nothing is on a shelf by the till and nothing is added to the bill.

Before you come in

Asked most weeks.

Do I have to book?

Not before two. Walk in and take the next gap. After two, and all day Saturday, the chair is booked, and a text with the service and a rough time gets you a slot the same day more often than not.

Do you cut women's hair?

Short cuts, fades, and undercuts, yes, and they are priced the same as everything on the wall. Anything long or coloured is better done by someone who does it all day, and the barber will tell you who.

Can I bring my kid?

Saturday mornings before eleven are for exactly that. Outside those hours a child who can sit still for twenty five minutes is welcome too, and one who cannot is not in trouble, it just means a shorter cut.

Because Hollis Barbershop does not exist. It is a fictional business built by actuallycoded as a portfolio concept, so this page carries no invented testimonials, star ratings, customer counts, or awards. Everything here demonstrates layout, copy, and interaction rather than a real trading record.
