On the bar

Six things, made properly.

The menu is short because a person who makes the same six drinks several hundred times makes them better than a person choosing between twenty. Prices include tax, and the tip jar is a jar, not a screen.

  • 01

    Batch brew

    Whichever filter coffee is on that morning, ground fresh and brewed by the litre. Refills cost a dollar, all day, and nobody checks how long you have been sitting there.

    $3.50
  • 02

    Espresso and milk

    Fieldnote No. 4, the house blend, pulled as a single or a double. Flat white, cortado, latte, and a cappuccino that actually has foam on it.

    $4.25

    Filter, by the cup

    Two single origins on at any time, brewed to order. It takes four minutes and it is worth them. Ask what the difference is and you will get a straight answer.

    $3 to $7

The room

A cafe you can actually stay in.

Plenty of places serve good coffee and then quietly make it awkward to remain in the building. The details below are the ones people actually ask about at the counter, so they are answered here instead.

  • OutletsAlong the window bench and under the long table. Deliberately not at the two seats by the door, which are for people staying ten minutes.
  • WifiFree, no password, no time limit, and nobody will come and ask whether you want anything else.
  • Long tableTen shared seats down the middle of the room. Sitting there means accepting the possibility of conversation.
  • DogsWelcome on the patio all day and inside before eleven, when the room is still quiet enough for it to be fair on them.
  • NoiseThe grinder runs on demand rather than continuously. If you are taking a call, the back corner is the quietest spot in the building.

Roasting

We roast in the back, twice a week, in small amounts.

Coffee is a fruit product with a short useful life. Roasting a fortnight of stock at once is easier to run and worse to drink, so Fieldnote does the harder version.

  1. We buy small and we say who from

    A few sacks at a time from importers who publish what they paid the producer. The bag tells you the farm, the region, and the process.

  2. Tuesday and Friday mornings

    The roaster runs before opening on two mornings a week. The room smells like it and there is nothing we can do about that.

  3. The date on the bag is a roast date

    Roasted on, not best before. Espresso is at its best from about a week after roasting; filter is good sooner. Both are past their best after a month.

  4. Wholesale is the same coffee

    Cafes and offices buying from us get what goes on our own bar. There is no separate cheaper blend for accounts, which is more common than it should be.

At the counter

Things people ask most days.

Can I work here all day?

Yes, and the room is set up for it: outlets where the long stays are, unmetered wifi, and refills at a dollar. The only ask is that the two seats by the door stay free for people grabbing a coffee and leaving, since those are the seats everyone wants for thirty seconds.

Do you sell the beans you serve?

Yes, in 250 gram and 1 kilogram bags, ground to order if you tell us what you brew on. It is the same coffee that goes through the bar that week, with the roast date on the label rather than a best before guess.

Is there anything for someone who does not like coffee?

Loose leaf tea by the pot and a hot chocolate that is worth ordering on its own terms. People are dragged in here by a partner constantly and the menu accounts for it.

Why are there no reviews, ratings, or awards on this page?

Because Fieldnote Coffee does not exist. It is a fictional business built by actuallycoded as a portfolio concept, so this page carries no invented testimonials, star ratings, customer counts, or awards. Everything here demonstrates layout, copy, and interaction rather than a real trading record.
