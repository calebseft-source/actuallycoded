What we look after

Four kinds of visit, and honest limits on all of them.

Northline is a general practice. Most of what walks through the door is routine, some of it is urgent, and a small amount belongs with a specialist. We say which is which on the first visit rather than the third.

Your first visit

An hour, and no treatment unless you ask for it.

A first appointment at Northline exists to produce a plan, not to start work. You will not be talked into anything while you are lying down with a light in your face.

  1. We talk before you sit back

    What is bothering you, what happened last time, what you can and cannot afford right now, and how long it has actually been.

  2. A proper look

    Photographs, the x-rays you are due, and gum measurements around every tooth. You see the pictures on the screen as they are taken.

  3. The plan, printed

    What needs doing now, what can safely wait, and what is only cosmetic. Each line carries its own price, so nothing is bundled.

  4. You choose the order

    Take the whole plan, take the urgent part, or take the printout home and think about it. All three are normal answers.

Timing. Plan for about an hour. If you arrive in pain, we treat the pain on that visit and leave the rest of the plan for another day.

If you have been putting this off

Anxiety is a normal reason to book, not a reason to apologise.

A good share of adults have not seen a dentist in years, and almost none of them enjoy admitting it. Say so when you book and the whole appointment is run differently.

  • Numbing gel goes on first A topical gel is applied and given a full two minutes to work before any injection. Most people never feel the needle.
  • A raised hand stops everything No explanation needed and no judgement afterwards. We stop, you breathe, we carry on when you say so.
  • Headphones, a blanket, sunglasses Bring your own playlist or borrow ours. The overhead light is the part people find hardest, so the glasses are there for a reason.
  • Nobody comments on the gap Ten years away gets the same tone as six months away. The only thing that matters is what we do from here.
  • You can book a talk with no exam Some people need to see the room and meet the dentist before anything goes near their mouth. That visit exists and it is free.

Before you book

The questions people actually ask.

What if I cannot afford the whole plan?

Then you do not do the whole plan. The printed plan separates what is urgent from what can wait precisely so it can be taken in pieces. A practice that will only treat you all at once is solving its own scheduling problem, not your dental one.

It has been years. Is it too late?

Almost never. Long gaps usually mean more to look at, not more to lecture about. The first visit maps what is there and sorts it by urgency, and a surprising amount of what looks alarming turns out to be cleaning and two fillings.

Will you tell me the price before you start?

Yes, in writing, itemised by line. If something changes once treatment is underway, work stops and you are asked before anything else is done. Nobody at Northline finds out what a visit cost by reading the bill afterwards.
