Where the money goes

What drives the price.

Pages. Every page is writing, layout and upkeep. A shop that needs people to call, book or visit needs one good page more than it needs eight thin ones. The cheapest honest site is the one with the fewest pages that still does the job.

The words. Most cheap options hand you a template and leave the writing to you, which is why so many local sites say "we strive to provide quality service." Writing that sounds like your shop, in words a customer would use, is the part that takes a person. Ask who is doing it.

Photos. Your own photos of the real place beat stock every time, and they cost you an afternoon with a phone. A site full of stock photos of strangers is the fastest way to look like everyone else.

Hosting and the domain. A domain is about $15 to $20 a year in your own name. Hosting a one page site is free or close to it. When a quote bundles these into a monthly fee, ask what the monthly is really paying for.

Changes. Every option charges for changes somehow: by the hour, by the month, or by a round included up front. Know which before you pay.

At any price

What you should get, whoever builds it.

  • A phone number that taps on a phone, in the first screen, and an address and hours that are right.
  • One clear thing to do: call, book, order or visit. Not a menu of twelve.
  • Words about your business, not about websites, and not about "solutions."
  • A page that loads in a couple of seconds on a phone on a bad connection, because that is where your customers are.
  • The domain in your name. If the designer registers it in theirs, you do not own your own address.
  • The files, or a way out. Whatever you pay, you should be able to leave with your site.

Before you pay

Five questions to ask any designer. Including me.

  1. Who writes the words? If the answer is "you send us the content," you are buying a layout, not a website.
  2. What do I own when it is done? The domain, the files, the hosting account. Get it in writing.
  3. What happens if I stop paying? If the site disappears, you were renting.
  4. What does a change cost after launch? By the hour, by the month, or included. Any answer is fine. No answer is not.
  5. Can I see three sites you built, on my phone, right now? Not screenshots. The real pages, loading on a phone. Mine are six concept pages for fictional businesses, built to the standard and labelled as such, until the first real one is live. Ask me that question and that is the honest answer you will get.
